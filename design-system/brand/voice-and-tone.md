# ACME Corp — Voice & Tone

ACME sounds like a veteran shopkeeper who has seen every product fail and ships
you the one that won't: **plainspoken, confident, dryly funny**.

## Voice principles

1. **Plainspoken.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No "leverage",
   "seamless", or "solutions".
2. **Confident, not boastful.** State what the product does; skip the
   adjectives. "Ships today" beats "blazingly fast logistics".
3. **Dry humor, rationed.** One wink per screen, never in errors about money,
   safety, or data loss.
4. **On the user's side.** Errors say what happened, why, and the next step —
   in that order. The system takes the blame when it's the system's fault.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing / empty states | Warm, playful | "Nothing here yet. The catalog has 40,000 ideas." |
| Transactional UI | Neutral, brisk | "Order #4417 confirmed. Arrives Thursday." |
| Errors (recoverable) | Calm, directive | "Card declined. Try another card or contact your bank." |
| Errors (destructive/irreversible) | Serious, zero humor | "This deletes 12 products permanently. This can't be undone." |
| Success | Brief, no confetti-speak | "Saved." |

## Writing rules

- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, labels. ("Track order", not
  "Track Order").
- Buttons are verb-first and specific: "Delete 12 products", not "Confirm" or
  "OK".
- Numbers as numerals: "3 items", never "three items".
- Dates absolute when it matters ("Arrives Jul 18"), relative when ambient
  ("Updated 2 hours ago").
- Never blame the user ("You entered an invalid…" → "That code isn't valid…").
- Avoid exclamation marks; one per flow, maximum, and only for genuinely good
  news.

## Vocabulary

| Say | Don't say |
| --- | --- |
| order | purchase transaction |
| ships / arrives | is fulfilled / is delivered to consignee |
| price | pricing solution |
| delete (permanent) / remove (reversible) | erase, destroy, trash |
| sign in / sign out | login, log off |
